# Chapter 2: Data Understanding and Exploratory Analysis

### 2.1 Dataset Provenance and Suitability
The empirical investigation utilizes the **Hourly PV Power and Dust Accumulation Dataset** collected at the Applied Research Unit for Renewable Energies (URAER) in Laghouat, Algeria ($33.8^\circ\text{N}$, $2.9^\circ\text{E}$, elevation $767\text{ m}$ above sea level). The dataset is published in the Mendeley Data repository under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0) with persistent identifier **DOI: `10.17632/4jdt83yy43.3`** (curated by Prof. Adel Mellit).

The experimental testbed features two identical SolarWorld SW-85 poly-crystalline photovoltaic modules tilted at $33^\circ$ (local latitude):
1. **Clean Reference Panel:** Manually cleaned every morning at sunrise using demineralized water.
2. **Naturally Soiled Panel:** Left uncleaned throughout the 6-month campaign, allowing natural atmospheric dust deposition, gravitational settling, and occasional wind/rain interactions.

This dataset is suitable for the research problem because it pairs synchronous meteorological measurements ($G_{POA}$, ambient temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation, cloud cover) with cell operating temperatures, periodic gravimetric dust density measurements, and electrical power generation from both panels across 4,368 consecutive hours (February 17 to August 17, 2022).

### 2.3 Target Definition and Data Availability
* **Target Definition ($P_{dusty\_W}$):** The prediction target is the continuous hourly electrical power output ($P_{dusty\_W}$, Watts) produced by the dust-affected SolarWorld SW-85 PV module. Accurately predicting this value enables operators to quantify expected generation deficits and schedule cleaning interventions.
* **Input Availability in Deployment:** When deployed in a commercial solar farm, input features must come from on-site automatic weather stations (irradiance, ambient temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, rain gauge) and module surface thermocouples ($T_{cell}$). Reconstructed dust density can be continuously estimated via aeolian deposition models or low-cost optical particulate sensors.
* **Exclusion of Clean Power ($P_{clean\_W}$):** Clean reference panel telemetry is quarantined. Because commercial solar installations do not deploy daily-cleaned twin reference panels across utility-scale arrays, using $P_{clean\_W}$ as an input would represent target leakage.

### 2.5 Exploratory Data Analysis and Key Patterns
1. **Diurnal Irradiance and Generation Curves:** Power output follows a unimodal diurnal bell curve peaking between 12:00 and 14:00. In-plane irradiance reaches $1,052.6\text{ W/m}^2$ under clear sky conditions, driving maximum clean power output to $80.29\text{ W}$.
2. **Dust Accumulation Dynamics:** Dust accumulates monotonically during dry spells. Over a 45-day dry window between late May and early July, reconstructed dust density rose steadily from $11.2\text{ g/m}^2$ to $21.4\text{ g/m}^2$.
3. **Rainfall Wash-Off Events:** Rainfall events ($>0.1\text{ mm}$, observed 29 times) act as natural surface washes, causing immediate decreases in dust density.
4. **Thermal Derating Saturation:** At solar noon, high insolation drives cell temperatures to $50^\circ\text{C} - 72^\circ\text{C}$. This induces thermal voltage losses that bend the power-irradiance trajectory downward, introducing non-linear behavior that linear models struggle to represent.

---

### 2.6 Insights That Shape the Methodology
* **Nighttime Removal:** Eliminating $G_{POA} \le 10\text{ W/m}^2$ avoids artificial metric inflation and allows error metrics to accurately reflect daytime operational losses.
* **Thermal Feature Engineering:** Ambient temperature alone has a low correlation ($r = +0.0657$) with output power, but the thermal gradient $\Delta T = T_{cell} - T_{amb}$ exhibits a strong correlation ($r = +0.9807$), justifying its creation.
* **Chronological Splitting:** Because weather patterns exhibit serial temporal autocorrelation, randomized train/test splitting causes look-ahead leakage. An expanding-window chronological split preserves real-world forecasting conditions.

### 7.1 Link Findings to the Grand Challenge
This project aligns directly with the **UTP Grand Challenge of Energy Security** and **UN Sustainable Development Goal 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy, Target 7.2)**:
* **Energy Security Mechanism:** In utility-scale solar farms, unmonitored dust accumulation degrades power output by an average of $21.2\%$ over prolonged dry periods, peaking at $24.8\%$ during midday generation. By predicting dust degradation in real time, asset operators can recover lost generation capacity without overspending on maintenance.

# Chapter 8: Limitations, Conclusion, and Future Work

### 8.1 Data and Methodological Limitations
Table 8.1 outlines key limitations, their impact on our conclusions, and practical responses.

#### Table 8.1: Methodological Limitations and Mitigation Strategies
| Limitation | Effect on Conclusions | Practical Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Geographic Site (Laghouat, Algeria)** | Models may not generalize directly to humid, tropical, or urban environments with different dust characteristics. | Re-tune model weights on local telemetry before deployment in other climate zones. |
| **Fixed $33^\circ$ Panel Tilt Angle** | Telemetry does not capture soiling on horizontal or vertically tracked arrays. | Incorporate panel tilt angle ($\beta$) and tracking orientation as input features in future campaigns. |
| **Reconstructed Dust Density (PCHIP)** | Hourly dust density relies on mathematical interpolation between 48 gravimetric samples. | Validate model using continuous real-time optical soiling monitors (e.g. optical transmission sensors). |
| **Model Drift Over Multi-Year Operation** | Long-term module aging (EVA yellowing, micro-cracking) was not observed over 6 months. | Implement continuous model retraining with rolling annual telemetry windows. |

### 8.2 Conclusion
This project developed and evaluated an empirical machine learning framework for predictive maintenance of solar photovoltaic panels affected by dust accumulation in semi-arid desert environments. Using 4,368 hours of multi-sensor telemetry from Laghouat, Algeria, the dataset was cleaned to 2,262 generating daylight hours. 

Evaluating Ridge Linear Regression, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost) through chronological 70/30 time-series partitioning and 5-fold cross-validation, the **XGBoost Regressor achieved the highest predictive accuracy** ($\text{MAE} = 1.24\text{ W}$, $\text{RMSE} = 1.85\text{ W}$, $R^2 = 0.9928$), delivering a $93.9\%$ error reduction over the baseline.

The empirical findings confirm that dust-induced soiling reduces peak module capacity by up to $21.08\text{ W}$ ($24.8\%$), while cell operating temperatures exceeding $50^\circ\text{C}$ introduce non-linear thermal derating. Implementing the recommended condition-based cleaning threshold ($\Delta \hat{P} \ge 10\text{ W}$ for $\ge 3$ consecutive peak hours) provides solar facility managers with an actionable method to maximize solar yields while reducing maintenance water use by up to $40\%$, directly supporting **UTP's Energy Security Grand Challenge** and **SDG 7 (Target 7.2)**.
